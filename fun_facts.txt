#1: General Choi Hong Hi, a South Korean army general and martial artist, is the Founder of Taekwondo. He is the established founder of TKD in ITF TKD, but his contributions are downplayed in WT TKD for political reasons tying back to the Cold War.
#2: When Choi Hong Hi, the founder of Taekwondo, originally sought funding and political support for his initiatives, he had to get it from either the military dictatorship of South Korea or the communist country of North Korea which put him in a tough spot. Eventually, he only briefly talked to North Korea about funding which branded him as a traitor, so South Korea separated themselves from him by creating the Kukkiwon style of TKD (the style used in World Taekwondo).
#3: By the late 1960s, there were many different styles of TKD. So, the South Korean government chartered the Korean Taekwondo Association to unify the various styles. This eventually formed the Kukkiwon style of TKD.
#4: Yong Chae Kim became the 5th president of the KTA in 1967 and is credited as having been the driving force behind the development of the Kukkiwon. However, it wasn’t until 1971, when Dr. Un Young Kim became the 6th president of the KTA, that Kukkiwon was finalized. Making Un Young Kim the first president of Kukkiwon.
#5: Kukkiwon and World Taekwondo are often used interchangeably, but they are not actually the same entities! The Kukkiwon is a government-sponsored national academy for taekwondo, while the WT is the international sports federation for Kukkiwon-style taekwondo. Both being headquartered in Seoul, however, it is not uncommon for the two organizations to collaborate closely.
#6: Before the 5 Tenets of Taekwondo, there were (and still are, although they are much less mentioned) the 11 Commandments of Taekwondo: Loyalty to your country, Respect your parents, Faithfulness to your spouse, Respect your brothers and sisters, Loyalty to your friends, Respect your elders, Respect your teachers, Never take life unjustly, Indomitable spirit, Loyalty to your school, Finish what you begin.
#7: The three largest Taekwondo associations in the world are World Taekwondo (founded formally in 1971), the International Taekwondo Federation (founded formally in 1966), and the American Taekwondo Association (founded in 1969).
#8: Taekwondo forms (referred to as Poomsae in WT and ATA-style, but Teul in ITF-style) are prearranged sequences of techniques useful in developing proper kinetics, mental and physical fortitude, and mushin, a Buddhist meditative state written as “no mind” in Chinese characters. There are also freestyle forms and weapons forms that serve the same purpose, but are also used for performative reasons.
#9: Each Taegeuk form is associated with a trigram. A trigram is three parallel lines, each line being either solid or broken. For example, the form Taegeuk Il Jang is associated with a trigram made up of three solid lines. This trigram represents "keon" meaning "the sky" or "the heavens."
#10: Taekwondo (태권도) means the way of the foot and fist. This comes from the Korean words Tae (태) meaning foot, Kwon (권) meaning fist or hand, and Do (도) meaning way of life.
#11: The Korean terminology for stance is seogi (서기). 
#12: The Korean terminology for kick is chagi (차기).
#13: The Korean terminology for block is makgi (막기).
#14: The Korean terminology for punch is jireugi (지르기).
#15: The Korean terminology for strike is chigi (치기).
#16: The Korean terminology for breaking is gyeokpa (격파).
#17: The Korean terminology for sparring is gyeorugi (겨루기) in WT or matseogi (맞서기) in ITF.
#18: There is a book published in 1994 called “Tae Kwon Do Dinosaurs: How Dinosaurs Train to Get Their Black Belts” by Yeon Hwa Park, Jeff Leibowitz, and Buho Choi..
#19: Here are some interesting Taekwondo books for you: Tae Kwon Do: The Ultimate Reference Guide to the World's Most Popular Martial Art, Encyclopedia of Taekwondo, A Killing Art: The Untold History of Taekwondo, Taekwondo: Ancient Wisdom for the Modern Warrior, The Taegeuk Cipher
#20: The roots of taekwondo can be traced back to the ancient Korean martial art practiced by the Hwarang, an elite warrior group of young men during the Silla Dynasty (57 BC–935 AD).
#21: During the Japanese occupation of Korea (1910–1945), Korean martial arts were suppressed, but practitioners continued to train secretly, incorporating some elements from Japanese martial arts, which can be seen in modern taekwondo.
#22: After Korea's liberation in 1945, five major martial arts schools, known as "kwans," were established. These kwans were key to the development of modern taekwondo.
#23: The term "taekwondo" was officially adopted in 1955, combining "tae" (foot), "kwon" (fist), and "do" (way) to mean "the way of the foot and fist."
#24: Taekwondo became an official Olympic sport in the 2000 Sydney Games, after being a demonstration sport in the 1988 Seoul and 1992 Barcelona Olympics.
#25: The first World Taekwondo Championships were held in Seoul, South Korea, in 1973, with 200 participants from 19 countries.
#26: Taekwondo emphasizes high, fast kicks, which are believed to develop flexibility, balance, and agility, setting it apart from other martial arts.
#27: Taekwondo's philosophy is based on five tenets: courtesy, integrity, perseverance, self-control, and indomitable spirit.
#28: The Korean term "hosinsul" (호신술) refers to self-defense techniques taught in taekwondo, focusing on practical applications of strikes, blocks, and holds.
#29: Taekwondo has a significant cultural impact in Korea, where it's not only a sport but also a mandatory part of the curriculum in many schools and universities.
#30: The Taekwondo Hall of Fame gives out the following awards to inductees: Humanitarian Award, Lifetime Achievement, Pioneer Instructor, Literary Excellence Award, Excellence in Leadership, Outstanding Referee, Coach of the Year, Pioneer Competitor, Competitor of the Year, and the Youth Excellence Award.
#31: World Taekwondo sometimes awards honorary black belts! Some honorary black belt holders include Barack Obama, Arnold Schwarzenegger, and Donald Trump. World Taekwondo also sometimes takes back these belts, as is the case of Vladimir Putin after the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine.
#32: For official Kukkiwon-certified black belt tests, 5th Dans (testing for 6th) and above must complete a thesis of varying topics in addition to their regular testing requirements. Those testing for 8th and 9th Dan must also complete an interview! Some thesis topics include a free topic for 6th Dan testers, the role of masters in Taekwondo globalization, the technical effect of training poomsae, and how Taekwondo affects your life and philosophical values.
#33: There are only three forms from the Taegeuks and black belt poomsae that do not end where they start: Il Jang (1 front stance behind), Keumgang (1 back stance ahead), and Jitae (1 front stance behind).
#34: There are only two forms that end with a left hand technique: Sam Jang and Yuk Jang (both left middle punch).
#35: The first ever move with an execution path unique to taekwondo was the low block. Other martial arts have low blocks, but some have differing execution paths such as TKD.
#36: Taekwondo is often associated with an old Korean martial art called Taekkyon due to President Syngman Ree calling it that at a demonstration. General Choi Hong Hi initially ran with it, but later admitted to interviewers that taekwondo did not originate from taekkyon.
